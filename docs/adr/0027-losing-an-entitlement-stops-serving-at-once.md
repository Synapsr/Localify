# Losing an Entitlement Stops Serving at Once

A URL stops serving as soon as the account behind it loses the right to serve it. That happens when the plan ends, the account is banned, or a downgrade puts the URL or the machine beyond the limits. Turning new requests away is not enough: the public path's checks only cover new requests, and a WebSocket or SSE stream already open would stay open for hours. Stopping is also not left to the daemon, because an old client or a modified one could keep its tunnel running.

The cloud stops what it controls. Once a refresh, a ban or a takedown removes the right to serve, the public path refuses new requests within its 10-second cache. The Worker then releases the affected tunnels: it drops their connections and deletes them, which closes every open stream. That runs right away, and the scheduled check runs it again if it failed, so the deadline is one sweep, 5 minutes. When only one URL of a machine loses its right, through a takedown or a suspension, while the machine keeps serving others, the tunnel cannot be kept: that URL's streams ride it too. The Worker therefore rotates the machine: it releases the current generation and lets the next one be provisioned. The URLs that keep their right are offline for those seconds, which is the price of cutting the others' streams for certain. The daemon is told why its runtime is blocked. It stops `cloudflared` and its public ingress, stays signed in, and says what to do: subscribe, update the card, free a serving slot, or remove URLs. A blocked runtime is final until the cause changes, unlike a cloud that cannot be reached, where the daemon keeps what it was already serving.

Nothing is deleted. URLs beyond the limits are suspended, with their slugs and passwords kept. Their owner can still list them, remove them, sign out and reach billing. Paying again, or removing URLs until the rest fits, brings them back without a new name. The URLs that keep serving after a downgrade are the oldest ones, by creation date, up to the limit. That choice is fixed and visible, so anyone can know in advance which URLs survive, and they choose by removing the others.
